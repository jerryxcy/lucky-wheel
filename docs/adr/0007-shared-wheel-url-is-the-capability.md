# The Shared Wheel URL is the capability

Anyone holding a Shared Wheel URL may view, mutate, and permanently delete that wheel, with no owner, account, separate administrator secret, short PIN, listing, or search endpoint in the MVP. There is consequently no Shared Wheel management dashboard, recent-wheel list, duplication, archive, restore, or Spin-record list: people retain a wheel by bookmarking its capability URL, reuse it by changing its roster or re-checking its members, and create another wheel when they need independent state. Manual deletion requires the expected wheel version and explicit UI confirmation, immediately hard-deletes the complete aggregate and Spin records in that transaction, notifies connected browsers after commit, and is not recoverable through the product. Independently, an enabled retention policy makes wheels expire by default 30 days after their last successful mutation so abandoned capability URLs do not accumulate indefinitely; deployments may change the duration or disable automatic expiry entirely, in which case `expiresAt` is null. Successful reads or open SSE connections do not extend retention. When retention is enabled, `expiresAt` is the authoritative access deadline: once reached, every operation treats the wheel as missing even if its rows await the next cleanup run. The scheduled cleanup later hard-deletes the complete aggregate and publishes a post-commit deletion notification; a successful mutation before the deadline calculates a new expiry. Deletion leaves no tombstone, so a manually deleted, expired, or never-existing wheel is uniformly `404 Not Found`; before deletion, `expiresAt` communicates the deadline. A randomly generated UUID v4 is both the wheel's database primary key and the opaque identifier in `/shared-wheels/{wheelId}`; its unpredictability prevents practical enumeration while avoiding a second public-token lifecycle that would only be needed for future link rotation. This deliberately treats disclosure of the URL as disclosure of full access. The MVP targets a trusted, self-hosted, single-instance service rather than a public multi-tenant SaaS, so public creation abuse controls, horizontal event distribution, and anonymous-user quotas are out of scope.
